Check your insurance coverage

  1. Open your insurer’s member portal. Search the current plan-year formulary for the exact brand, formulation and prescribed indication.
  2. Call the pharmacy benefits number on your card. Ask whether the product is covered for that indication, whether prior authorization or step therapy applies, and which pharmacies are in network.
  3. Request an estimate for a 28-day fill. Ask how the deductible, copay or coinsurance affects the first fill and later refills. Record the reference number and date.
  4. If coverage is denied, request the written reason, the exception or appeal instructions and the deadline. Share the letter with your prescriber.

    Selected safety readings

    Three selected studies. Open a question for its population, comparison and limitations.

    Semaglutide · How common was nausea?

    Across three STEP trials, 2,117 adults with overweight or obesity were assigned semaglutide and 1,262 placebo for 68 weeks. Nausea was reported by 43.9% and 16.1%, respectively. These pooled rates describe the trial populations and follow-up period. Read the pooled analysis

    Tirzepatide and semaglutide · What did routine care records show?

    A multicenter observational study included 1,115 patients with obesity receiving tirzepatide and 1,434 receiving semaglutide. At least one adverse event was recorded for 51.0% and 50.9%, respectively. Treatment selection and event recording can differ between groups, so similar percentages do not establish equivalent safety. Read the observational study

    Retatrutide · What did the phase 2 trial observe?

    Researchers assigned 338 adults with obesity or overweight and a weight-related condition to retatrutide or placebo for 48 weeks. Gastrointestinal symptoms were the most frequent adverse events in the retatrutide groups. Heart rate increases peaked around week 24. This sample and follow-up give limited information about rare or longer-term harms. Read the phase 2 trial
